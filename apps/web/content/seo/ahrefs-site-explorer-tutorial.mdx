# Introduction to Ahrefs Site Explorer

## What is Ahrefs Site Explorer?

Ahrefs Site Explorer is a comprehensive SEO analysis tool that pulls detailed insights into any website's organic performance. Following the ahrefs site explorer tutorial, you'll learn how to evaluate a site's organic keywords, identify its top-performing pages, and analyze the backlink profile that drives authority. Ahrefs recommends selecting HTTP + HTTPS for most searches and choosing your search scope—subdomains, domain, path, or exact URL—before interpreting results. This foundational setup ensures accurate, actionable data across all reports.

## Key Features Overview

Site Explorer delivers four critical data points for SEO professionals:

1. **Organic Keywords Report** – Track all keywords a domain ranks for with search volume and traffic estimates
2. **Top Pages Report** – Identify which pages generate the most organic traffic
3. **Backlinks Report** – Analyze inbound links and referring domains
4. **Organic Competitors Report** – Discover sites ranking for similar keywords

The Organic Competitors feature is particularly powerful for identifying content gaps. Ahrefs recommends filtering for a Keyword Difficulty score of 10 or less when hunting lower-competition opportunities your competitors rank for but you don't.

# Getting Started with Ahrefs Site Explorer

Ahrefs Site Explorer is a powerful SEO tool that reports on a site's organic keywords, top pages, and backlinks. Whether you're auditing your own site or researching competitors, understanding how to set up and navigate Site Explorer is essential for uncovering content and SEO opportunities.

## Navigating the Dashboard

Once logged in, locate the Site Explorer tool from the main menu. The dashboard presents several key reports:

- **Organic keywords** — Shows which search terms drive traffic to a site
- **Top pages** — Ranks pages by traffic potential
- **Backlinks** — Displays referring domains and link authority

Each report is filterable and sortable, allowing you to customize your analysis.

## Selecting Search Scope: Domain, Subdomain, or URL

Before running reports, you must choose your search scope. Ahrefs recommends selecting **HTTP + HTTPS** for most searches to capture all traffic data. Then select your scope type:

1. **Domain** — Analyzes the entire domain (e.g., example.com)
2. **Subdomain** — Focuses on a specific subdomain (e.g., blog.example.com)
3. **Path** — Narrows to a specific directory (e.g., example.com/resources)
4. **Exact URL** — Examines a single page

Choose the scope that matches your research objective. Domain-level searches work best for broad competitive analysis, while URL-level searches suit detailed page audits.

## Analyzing Organic Keywords

# Analyzing Organic Keywords

## Understanding Organic Keywords Report

Ahrefs Site Explorer delivers detailed reports on a site's organic keywords, top-performing pages, and backlink profiles. Before diving into interpretation, Ahrefs recommends configuring two critical settings:

1. **Protocol Selection**: Choose HTTP + HTTPS for most searches to capture all traffic variants
2. **Search Scope**: Select from subdomains, domain, path, or exact URL depending on your analysis needs

These settings ensure your organic keyword data reflects the complete picture of ranking performance.

## Using the Keyword Difficulty Filter

The Keyword Difficulty (KD) filter is your primary tool for prioritizing opportunities. Ahrefs Starter, which includes Site Explorer, is **$29/month**.

To identify quick-win opportunities:

1. Navigate to Site Explorer → Organic competitors
2. Input your domain and review competing sites
3. Cross-reference competitor keywords against your own rankings
4. Filter results to **KD ≤ 10** for lower-difficulty topics

This approach reveals content gaps—keywords your competitors rank for that your site doesn't yet target.

## Identifying Low-Difficulty Topics

Ahrefs recommends examining competitor **Site structure** and **Top pages** reports to uncover content opportunities systematically. When you identify high-potential keywords, analyze the competitor's top-ranking page for that term to understand content format, depth, and structure.

For sites managing existing content, Ahrefs' tutorial emphasizes content recovery: if you have broken pages with incoming backlinks, filter your reports using "Best by links" → HTTP 404. You can then either restore the page or implement a 301 redirect to a relevant live page, preserving SEO equity.

**Practical Example:**
If a competitor ranks for "sustainable packaging solutions" (KD: 8) and you don't, that keyword becomes a content opportunity. Check their top page's word count, heading structure, and covered subtopics. Your content should match or exceed these benchmarks while offering unique insights.

This systematic approach—combining competitor analysis, difficulty filtering, and content auditing—transforms organic keyword data into actionable SEO strategy. ## Exploring Backlinks and Competitors

# Exploring Backlinks and Competitors

Ahrefs Site Explorer is the cornerstone tool for understanding your domain's organic visibility. The platform reports on a site's organic keywords, top pages, and backlinks—providing a complete picture of your search presence.

Before diving into reports, follow these setup steps:

1. Enter your domain URL into Site Explorer
2. Select **HTTP + HTTPS** for most searches (Ahrefs recommends this to capture all protocol variations)
3. Choose your search scope: subdomains, domain, path, or exact URL
4. Let the data populate before interpreting results

This foundational configuration ensures you're analyzing accurate, complete data rather than fragmented reports.

## Identifying Organic Competitors

Ahrefs Site Explorer's **Organic Competitors report** reveals which sites rank for similar keywords to yours. This is where competitive gaps emerge.

To find low-hanging keyword opportunities:

- Open the Organic Competitors report for your domain
- Identify 3–5 competing sites with similar traffic profiles
- Apply a **Keyword Difficulty filter of 10 or less** to surface easier ranking targets
- Cross-reference: which keywords do *they* rank for that *you don't*?

These lower-difficulty topics represent quick wins—they're already proving valuable to your audience segment, but your site hasn't captured them yet.

**Broken Page Recovery**: Don't overlook your own technical wins. If your site has pages with backlinks that return HTTP 404 errors, filter for "Best by links" and either restore the page or redirect it to relevant live content. ## Finding Content Opportunities

# Finding Content Opportunities

## Examining Top Pages Report

Ahrefs Site Explorer reports on a site's organic keywords, top pages, and backlinks. Before diving into reports, select **HTTP + HTTPS** for most searches and choose your search scope—subdomains, domain, path, or exact URL—to ensure accurate data interpretation.

The Top Pages report reveals which content drives the most organic traffic. Pairing this with the Organic Competitors report identifies gaps in your strategy. Ahrefs recommends:

1. Enter a competitor domain into Site Explorer
2. Navigate to the Organic Competitors report
3. Examine keywords competitors rank for that your site doesn't
4. Apply a Keyword Difficulty filter of **10 or less** to prioritize lower-competition topics
5. Cross-reference these opportunities against your own Top Pages report

This workflow uncovers quick wins—topics where ranking is achievable and competitors already validate demand.

## Checking Site Structure for Gaps

Site structure analysis reveals content silos and missing topics. Use Ahrefs' Site Structure report alongside the Top Pages report to spot patterns. If competitors rank for multiple related keywords but your site lacks content on those themes, you've found an opportunity cluster.

Look for:
- **Category pages** with weak link authority
- **Topic clusters** where competitors have 5+ pages and you have 0–1
- **Cornerstone content** gaps that should anchor your strategy

## Restoring Broken Pages with Backlinks

Broken pages with existing backlinks represent untapped ranking potential. Ahrefs Starter is **$29/month** and Lite is **$129/month**. Paid plans include the core Site Explorer reports, including broken link detection.

To recover lost equity:

1. Log into Site Explorer and analyze your own domain
2. Go to Best by Links and filter to **HTTP 404** status
3. Identify broken pages with the highest backlink counts
4. Either restore the original page or redirect it to a relevant live page using a 301 redirect

This approach preserves link equity and signals. A page that previously ranked and generated backlinks can recover quickly once restored, often outperforming fresh content.

Combining these three tactics—examining competitor performance, mapping site structure, and recovering broken pages—builds a data-driven content roadmap that balances quick wins with strategic growth.
